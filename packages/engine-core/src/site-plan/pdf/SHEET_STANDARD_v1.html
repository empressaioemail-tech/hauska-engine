SITE PLAN SHEET STANDARD

Twenty-one rules, a token map and an acceptance checklist for the generated three-page site-plan PDF. Written against the output of 2026-07-27 (parcels 48021:34785 and qa2:dense-small); every rule below exists because that output broke it.

v1.1 (2026-07-28): adds §21 · VERTICAL RHYTHM — the metrics-driven line-box model that keeps type off the rules above it.

1 · SCOPE AND SHEET ORDER

Every parcel produces exactly three Letter-portrait pages, in this order and never a different count:

SheetContentCarries
1 of 3DrawingParcel ring, buildable envelope, setbacks, contours, street, dimension tags, legend, scale bar, north arrow, lot-area callout
2 of 3SummaryTwo-column key/value data under three group headings, the segment table when tags were moved off sheet 1, provenance table
3 of 3AerialDuotone orthophoto backdrop, vector overlay of the same ring geometry, imagery provenance strip

A sheet is never omitted. If its data is unavailable the sheet still prints, carrying the honest-absence treatment described in §5, §6 and §17.

2 · THE TWENTY-ONE RULES

§1 · SHEET
Letter portrait, 8.5×11 in, ground neutral-100. Page margins 48 / 46 / 36 px at 96 dpi; inner gaps from the spacing scale. No bleed. Content never exceeds the page box — see §13.
§2 · HEADER, EVERY PAGE
Accent kicker (11px, 0.22em, uppercase, naming the sheet and its number) → street address in Barlow Condensed 600 uppercase 30px → one 13px line of city, parcel and county. Right: three stat columns in Barlow Condensed 600 22px. A 1px full-ink rule closes the header. No frame, no title strip, no boxed block. No empty-field placeholders — never print “Date: not on file”; omit the field or print the generated stamp.
§3 · LAYER ORDER AND WEIGHT
contour → contour labels → envelope fill → setback dash → property line → dimension tags → sheet furniture. The property line is the heaviest stroke on the sheet; nothing may match or exceed it. One accent hue only. Colour never carries meaning alone — every layer also differs in weight or dash.
§4 · LABEL COLLISION CASCADE
Bearing/distance tags sit outside the ring, one per segment, rotated to the segment, centred on its midpoint, distance first. On collision, in order: (a) shorten to distance only; (b) move to a margin leader (§16); (c) drop from the drawing and tabulate on sheet 2. Never stack, never duplicate. Setback labels print once per unique value per side; identical adjacent values collapse to “SIDE 5' (typ.)”. Suppress any label on an edge shorter than 3× the label. A setback label must clear the dash it labels — if the gap between ring and envelope is narrower than the label, place it inside the envelope. Contour labels: one per contour, in the left margin, never inside the envelope.
§5 · EMPTY LAYERS MUST LOOK INTENTIONAL
Every layer appears in the legend, in fixed order, populated or not. An empty layer greys to neutral-500 with its reason inline on the same row (“Street — layer empty · no road node attaches”). Never delete the row, never leave a blank swatch, never draw substitute geometry, never dump the reason as a floating paragraph on the drawing.
§6 · UNAVAILABLE VALUES
A solid ink UNAVAILABLE chip plus one plain sentence of reason, sentence case, ≤12 words. Never “N/A”, never a bare lowercase “unavailable”, never a hyphen where an em dash belongs, never a fabricated value. Fixture or provisional values keep their real value and take a FIXTURE LABEL chip — identical wording every time, no inline parenthetical, no second spelling of the same state.
§7 · SUMMARY PAGE
Two-column key/value on a 200px label column, hairline rule per row, grouped under PARCEL, ZONING & BUILDABILITY, SITE CONDITIONS in that order, max 5 rows each; group headings in accent, 10.5px, 0.2em. Values carry their unit plus at most one grey qualifier inline; qualifiers never become their own row. Numbers tabular. Provenance follows as a three-column table — layer, source, confidence — one row per layer, never four stacked lines.
§8 · FINE PRINT
Bottom of each page, 8.2px, neutral-600, one paragraph. Always: derived from public GIS records / not a boundary survey / not for legal record / tags GIS-approximate. Honest-absence, synthetic-fixture, moved-tag and imagery-age disclosures append to that same paragraph. No callout boxes on the drawing.
§9 · SHEET FURNITURE
Exactly one north arrow, top-right inside the drawing area: filled triangle over a condensed “N”, no compass rose, no cardinal text. Exactly one scale bar, bottom-right: three segments, three labels, unit on the last one (0 · 33 · 66 ft) — never the unit on its own line. Legend bottom-left on the same baseline, two columns. Lot-area callout centred in the envelope, condensed uppercase over a grey qualifier; suppress it if the envelope is narrower than 40 label-widths.
§10 · NEVER
No fabricated geometry. No rounded corners, no drop shadows in print, no gradients, no emoji, no second accent hue, no italic body text, no hard-coded hex, font name or px where a token exists.
§11 · PROSE HYGIENE
Nothing on the sheet may contain a machine identifier, a colon-delimited code, a nested parenthesis, or a clause that repeats a source name. Every reason is one sentence, sentence case, ends in a period, ≤12 words. Every value carries at most one grey qualifier. Machine codes (setback-consumes-lot, front-edge-hint) appear only in the provenance source column.
§12 · NUMBER AND UNIT FORM
Thousands separator on every quantity ≥ 1,000. Feet take a prime (15'), inches a double prime, metres a space and “m”. Areas read “sq ft”, never “sqft” or “ft²”. Scale reads 1" = 29', never 1" = 29 ft. Ranges take a spaced en dash, negations an em dash. Acreage to two decimals in parentheses after square feet. All figures tabular-lined.
§13 · NO TRUNCATION, NO OVERFLOW
If a string does not fit its box, shorten the content upstream — never clip the glyphs. If a page's content exceeds the page box, restructure it (split a table into columns, fold a column into a caption, tighten block gaps) — never let it spill or scroll. Confidence is a fixed short enum: asserted · rule · sample · centerline accurate · honest absence · honest unavailable, plus at most one qualifier such as “· GIS-approx”. Long narrative belongs in the fine print, which wraps.
§14 · DRAW-ONCE GUARANTEE
Each layer renders in exactly one pass. One tag per segment, one label per unique setback value per side, one north arrow, one scale bar, one legend. If a pass may run twice, key every mark by segment or layer id and drop duplicates before drawing — a doubled tag is worse than a missing one.
§15 · DEGENERATE GEOMETRY
When the inward offset collapses, draw no envelope and no setback lines. State it three ways and nowhere else: the BUILDABLE header stat reads NONE in neutral-500; a centred callout inside the ring reads “NO BUILDABLE ENVELOPE” over one sentence of reason; the envelope and setback legend rows grey out with their reason inline. Sheet 2 carries an UNAVAILABLE chip on buildable area. Never draw a zero-width or inverted polygon.
§16 · MARGIN LEADERS AND THE SEGMENT TABLE
Cascade tier (b) draws a 0.13-unit neutral-500 leader from the segment midpoint to the nearest sheet margin, ending in “Sn · distance”. Leaders never cross each other or the ring. Any segment whose tag was shortened, moved or dropped appears in the sheet-2 segment table with its full bearing, its distance and what happened to it on sheet 1; the fine print says the tags were moved. A dropped tag with no table row is a defect.
§17 · IMAGERY IS A BACKDROP
The aerial sheet illustrates context; it never carries a measurement. No dimension, area, bearing or setback figure may be derived from the raster. The fine print says so in one sentence and names sheet 1 as governing where the two disagree. If imagery is missing, print the sheet with the overlay on the paper ground and an UNAVAILABLE chip in the imagery strip — do not drop the page.
§18 · ONE GEOMETRY, TWO SHEETS
The ring and envelope on the aerial sheet are drawn from the same coordinates and the same viewBox as sheet 1 — never re-projected, re-simplified or re-fit to the image. State the registration tolerance in the header stat and again in the fine print; if it cannot be computed, the stat takes an UNAVAILABLE chip rather than a guessed number. Distances over imagery are distance-only; full bearings stay on sheet 1.
§19 · IMAGERY PROVENANCE STRIP
Four cells above the fine print, always all four, in this order: source · capture date · resolution · registration. Capture date carries its age in months as a grey qualifier; imagery older than 24 months adds one sentence to the fine print saying conditions may have changed. Each missing cell takes an UNAVAILABLE chip with its reason — never an empty cell, never an inferred date.
§20 · DUOTONE AND LEGIBILITY OVER RASTER
Imagery passes through the duotone treatment so it desaturates into the accent — never full colour, never a second hue. Nothing is burned into the raster: every mark is vector, above the image. Type and the ring take a paper-coloured outline behind them (paint-order stroke, 0.9–1.1 units) so they hold over any tone. The frame keeps its registration marks. Contours are omitted here and the legend says where to find them.
§21 · VERTICAL RHYTHM
All spacing derives from the base unit 3.4px (--space-1) scale, and every text row against a rule is a LINE BOX, never a bare baseline. The row model is: rule → padding-top (space token) → line box(es) → padding-bottom → next rule. A line box is line-height × size tall with the glyph content area (ascent − descent, from real font metrics) centred in it; text draws at its baseline INSIDE the box, so the cap tops clear the rule above by padding-top plus half-leading plus (ascent − cap-height) — computed from the embedded font's unitsPerEm / ascent / descent / capHeight, never from an eyeballed offset. Multi-line values wrap within the row, growing it one line box per line; chips centre vertically on the row's line box. PROXIMITY: a group heading belongs to its rows — heading padding-bottom is space-2 to its first rule, while the gap to the section above is ≥ space-6; the heading must always sit closer to its own rows than to what precedes it.

5 · ACCEPTANCE CHECKLIST

Run on both fixtures before grading. Any single failure fails the sheet.

Three pages, in order, none omitted. No mark drawn twice: count tags, setback labels, north arrows, scale bars, legends. No label overlaps another label, the ring, or the envelope dash it describes. Every drawing string renders at 10 px or more on the 816×1056 sheet. No string clipped mid-word anywhere, and no page content past the page box. No machine identifier, colon code or nested parenthesis outside the provenance source column. Legend lists every layer, greyed with an inline reason where empty; no floating note paragraphs. Every unavailable value has a chip and one sentence ≤12 words; no “N/A”, no empty cell. Thousands separators, prime marks and “sq ft” correct on every figure. Moved or dropped tags all appear in the sheet-2 segment table, and the fine print says so. Degenerate parcels state the collapse exactly three times and draw no envelope. Aerial overlay uses sheet 1's coordinates; registration tolerance printed in the header and fine print. Imagery strip carries all four cells; imagery over 24 months old disclosed in the fine print. Fine print on all three pages carries the four standing disclaimers. No colour outside the steel accent and the neutral ramp; no hard-coded hex or font name.

6 · REFERENCE SHEETS

Pixel references accompany this standard: 48021:34785 as the three-page gold set (drawing, summary, aerial), and qa2:dense-small as the degenerate case — eight segments, no buildable envelope, cascade tiers (a) and (b) both exercised, overflow tabulated on sheet 2. Where the standard and a reference sheet disagree, the standard governs and the reference is the defect.
